A frozen pipe does not usually burst while it is frozen. It bursts when it thaws, or when pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet. That is why the phone rings the morning after the cold snap, not during it.

## Where pipes freeze in Central Pennsylvania homes

We see the same locations over and over:

- **Exterior walls.** Kitchen sinks on outside walls are the number one location, especially in older Johnstown and Altoona homes with little wall insulation.
- **Unheated basements and crawl spaces.** Stone-foundation basements in the region can drop below freezing during a long cold stretch.
- **Garages.** Water lines running through or above an attached garage, especially to a laundry room or bathroom above it.
- **Attics.** Supply lines to upstairs bathrooms routed through unconditioned attic space.
- **Outdoor hose bibs** that were not disconnected in the fall.
- **Vacant or seasonal homes** where the heat was turned down or off.

## Prevention that actually works

**Before winter**

- Disconnect and drain garden hoses. Shut off and drain interior valves to exterior spigots if you have them.
- Insulate exposed pipes in basements, crawl spaces and garages with foam sleeves. It is cheap and takes an afternoon.
- Seal air leaks near pipes: gaps around dryer vents, hose bibs, cable penetrations and sill plates. Cold air blowing directly on a pipe is worse than cold air in general.
- Know where your main water shutoff is and make sure it turns.

**During a cold snap (below 15°F)**

- Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so heated air reaches the pipes.
- Let a faucet on the far side of the house drip slightly. Moving water is much harder to freeze, and an open faucet relieves pressure if ice does form.
- Keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night. The savings from a nighttime setback are not worth a burst pipe.
- Keep garage doors closed.

**If you leave for more than a couple of days**

- Keep heat no lower than 55°F.
- Better yet, shut off the main and open the lowest faucet to drain the lines.
- Have a neighbor check the house.

## If a pipe is frozen but not burst

You will know because a faucet gives a trickle or nothing at all. Leave that faucet open. Warm the pipe gradually with a hair dryer, a heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water, starting at the faucet end and working toward the frozen section. Never use a torch, propane heater or any open flame. If you cannot find or reach the frozen section, call a plumber before it thaws on its own.

## If a pipe has burst

1. **Shut off the main water valve immediately.** Every minute counts.
2. **Turn off the electricity** to the affected area if water is near outlets, appliances or the panel.
3. **Open faucets** to relieve pressure and drain the lines.

5. **Photograph everything** before moving it.
6. **Call a plumber** for the repair. We work alongside local plumbers constantly and can recommend one.

## Why "it is just water" is the expensive mistake

A burst pipe puts clean water into a house, which sounds harmless. But clean water in a wall cavity or under a floor for more than a day or two becomes a mold problem, and mold is where insurance coverage gets tight. Professional extraction and structural drying within the first 24 hours is the difference between a two-week job and a two-month one.

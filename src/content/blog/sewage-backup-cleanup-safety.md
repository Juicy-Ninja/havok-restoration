Every spring and after every major thunderstorm, our Johnstown line gets the same call: "There is water coming up the floor drain." Sometimes the homeowner has already been down there in sneakers with a shop vac. Please read this before you do that.

## Why sewage water is different from a burst pipe

Restoration professionals classify water in three categories:

- **Category 1 (clean):** supply lines, ice maker lines, rainwater. Safe to touch.
- **Category 2 (gray):** dishwasher, washing machine, toilet overflow with urine only. Contaminated, can cause illness.
- **Category 3 (black):** sewage, floodwater from rivers or streets, toilet overflow with feces, water that has sat more than 48 hours. Grossly contaminated.

A sewer backup is Category 3 from the moment it appears. It carries bacteria (E. coli, salmonella), viruses (hepatitis A, norovirus), parasites, and whatever chemicals went down every drain upstream. It is a biohazard, and it is treated with the same protocols we use for trauma scenes.

## What to do immediately

1. **Stay out of the water.** Do not walk through it, especially with open cuts. If you must, wear rubber boots, gloves and eye protection.
2. **Stop using water in the house.** Every flush, shower and load of laundry adds to the backup.
3. **Turn off electricity to the basement** if water is anywhere near outlets, the furnace or the panel. If the panel is in the basement and you would need to stand in water to reach it, call the utility instead.
4. **Keep kids and pets upstairs.**
5. **Call us at (814) 809-9833.** Then call a plumber to clear the line. Both need to happen, and we coordinate with local plumbers every week.
6. **Photograph the water level and affected items** from the stairs.

## What can be saved and what cannot

Category 3 water changes the salvage math. The IICRC S500 standard is clear about porous materials:

**Must be discarded:**
- Carpet and carpet pad
- Drywall and insulation that contacted the water (typically cut 12 to 24 inches above the water line)
- Particleboard furniture and cabinets
- Mattresses, upholstered furniture, pillows
- Cardboard boxes and paper contents
- Food, cosmetics, medications

**Can usually be cleaned and disinfected:**
- Concrete floors and block walls
- Framing lumber
- Metal shelving and tools
- Hard plastic bins (contents evaluated individually)
- Washable clothing, laundered hot with disinfectant
- Solid wood furniture, depending on exposure

Throwing out a finished basement's drywall feels drastic. It is also the only way to make the space safe. Sewage soaks into the paper face and gypsum and cannot be disinfected.

## How professional cleanup works

- **Containment.** We isolate the basement from the rest of the house so contaminated air does not migrate upstairs.
- **PPE.** Our technicians work in Tyvek suits, respirators, gloves and boots.
- **Extraction.** Sewage and solids are pumped out and disposed of properly.
- **Removal.** Contaminated porous materials are bagged and removed under containment.
- **Cleaning.** Every remaining surface is pressure washed or scrubbed, then treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectant.
- **Drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the structure hits dry standard.
- **Verification.** ATP testing confirms surfaces are actually clean, not just visibly clean.
- **Deodorization and rebuild.** Odor is removed at the source, then the basement is rebuilt.

## Preventing the next one

- **Install a backwater valve** on your main sewer line. It closes automatically when flow reverses. This is the single best protection for a finished basement in an older neighborhood.
- **Have your lateral camera-inspected** if your home is more than 40 years old. Root intrusion and pipe collapse can be fixed before they back up.
- **Never pour grease down the drain.**
- **Add a sewer backup endorsement** to your homeowners policy. Standard policies exclude this, and the endorsement is cheap.

The adjuster says, "We have a company we work with. They can be there tomorrow." It sounds helpful, and sometimes it is. But you should know what you are agreeing to.

## Your right to choose

In Pennsylvania, you have the right to hire the contractor of your choice to repair your home after a covered loss. Your insurance carrier can recommend a vendor. It cannot require you to use one, and it cannot reduce your claim payment because you chose someone else. The claim is based on the policy and the scope of damage, not on who does the work.

Carriers know this. The recommendation is usually phrased as a convenience, and it is a convenience, for them.

## How preferred vendor programs work

Insurance carriers maintain networks of restoration contractors, often through a third-party administrator. The contractor agrees to the carrier's pricing, documentation rules, and timelines, and in exchange receives a steady stream of claims. Many good companies participate. Many good companies do not.

The concern is not that network contractors are bad. It is that their customer, in a business sense, is the carrier. When there is a judgment call about whether a wet wall needs to be opened or whether a floor can be dried in place, the network contractor has a relationship to protect. An independent contractor's only relationship is with you.

## What to ask any restoration contractor

Whoever you hire, network or not, ask these questions:

1. **Are you IICRC certified?** Which certifications? Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) are the baseline for water losses.
2. **Are you a registered Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor?** Ask for the HIC number. You can verify it on the Attorney General's website.
3. **Are you insured?** Ask for a certificate of general liability and workers compensation. If a worker gets hurt in your home and the company is uninsured, it can become your problem.
4. **Do you write estimates in Xactimate?** It is the industry standard and what your adjuster uses. Estimates in other formats cause delays and disputes.
5. **Who is my single point of contact?** You should have one person's name and cell number.
6. **Do you do the reconstruction, or just mitigation?** Splitting the job between two companies means two estimates, two schedules and a gap where nobody is accountable.
7. **How do you handle disagreements with the adjuster?** The right answer involves documentation and supplements, not "we just do what they approve."
8. **Can I see recent local references?**

## Red flags

- Shows up at your door uninvited after a storm or fire
- Wants you to sign an "authorization" or "assignment of benefits" on the spot before explaining it
- Offers to waive or "cover" your deductible (this is insurance fraud in Pennsylvania)
- Cannot produce insurance certificates or a HIC number
- Pressures you to decide today
- Has out-of-state plates and a local phone number that was activated last month

## Protecting yourself

- **Read before you sign.** A work authorization for emergency mitigation is normal. A contract for full reconstruction should come after the scope is agreed.
- **Keep your own records.** Photos, dates, names, claim number, every email.
- **Ask for the moisture readings.** Any competent water restoration company documents daily readings. You are entitled to see them.
- **Do not pay the full amount up front.** Deposits are normal for uninsured work. Full prepayment is not.

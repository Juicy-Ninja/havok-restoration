We meet a lot of Central Pennsylvania homeowners at the worst moment to be learning what their policy says. So here is the primer we wish everyone had read before the water showed up.

This is general information based on typical HO-3 policies sold in Pennsylvania. Your policy controls. Read it, and call us if you want help understanding it.

## The key phrase: "sudden and accidental"

Most homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental, and exclude damage that is gradual, expected or caused by neglect. That single distinction decides most claims.

| Usually covered | Usually excluded |
|---|---|
| Burst supply pipe | Slow leak under the sink for months |
| Water heater tank failure | Corroded pipe that "finally let go" after years of drips |
| Washing machine hose rupture | Seepage through foundation walls |
| Ice dam causing roof leak | Flooding from rivers, creeks or surface water |
| Toilet supply line failure | Sewer or drain backup (without rider) |
| Firefighting water after a fire | Damage from failure to maintain the home |

## Burst pipes and frozen pipes

Covered in nearly every case, with one common exception: if the home was unoccupied and you did not maintain heat or shut off the water, many carriers exclude freeze damage. If you winter in Florida and leave a Westmont house empty, keep the heat on and have someone check it.

## Sewer and drain backups

This is the one that surprises people. A standard policy excludes water that backs up through sewers, drains or sump pumps. Given how many Johnstown, Altoona and Greensburg homes have older combined sewers and basement floor drains, this exclusion matters.

The fix is a **sewer backup endorsement** (sometimes called water backup coverage). It typically costs a small amount per year and usually provides a set amount of coverage. If you have a finished basement, ask your agent about it today.

## Flooding

Homeowners insurance does not cover flooding. Period. Flood means water from outside the home: rivers, creeks, flash floods, storm surge, or heavy rain pooling and entering. In the Conemaugh Valley that is not a theoretical risk.

Flood coverage comes from the **National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)** or a private flood policy. Johnstown's history is the reason a lot of local mortgages require it in mapped flood zones. If you are not in a mapped zone, you can still buy it, and it is usually inexpensive outside high-risk areas.

## Ice dams and roof leaks

Interior water damage from an ice dam is usually covered, because the water entry is sudden. The roof repair itself may or may not be, depending on whether the carrier considers it wear or storm damage. Long-term roof leaks that stained a ceiling over years are typically excluded as maintenance.

## Mold

Mold coverage in Pennsylvania is usually limited. Most policies cover mold only when it results from a covered water loss, and many cap it at a sub-limit. Mold from humidity, chronic leaks or neglect is excluded. This is one more reason fast professional drying matters: it keeps a covered water claim from turning into an excluded mold problem.

## Contents versus structure

Your policy has separate coverage for the building (Coverage A) and your belongings (Coverage C). Furniture, electronics, clothing and rugs damaged by a covered water event are usually covered, often at replacement cost if you selected that option. Keep receipts and photos.

## Additional living expenses

If the home is unlivable during a covered loss, most policies pay for a hotel or rental and extra food costs under Loss of Use coverage. Ask your adjuster about it up front.

## What you are required to do

Every policy includes a duty to mitigate. You must take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. That means shutting off the water, calling a restoration company promptly, and not letting a wet basement sit for a week. Carriers can and do reduce claims where the homeowner waited.

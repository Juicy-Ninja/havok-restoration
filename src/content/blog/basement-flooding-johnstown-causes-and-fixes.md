Johnstown is a valley town built on rivers, with steep hillsides on every side and neighborhoods full of homes that predate modern drainage. That geography, plus a century of coal and steel infrastructure, makes basement water an everyday fact of life here. But "my basement floods" can mean four different things, and each has a different fix.

## 1. Sump pump failure

**How to tell:** Water rising from the sump pit, usually during or after heavy rain, in a basement that is normally dry.

**Why it happens:** The pump lost power (storms take out power and dump rain at the same time), the float switch stuck, the pump wore out, or the discharge line froze or clogged. In Westmont, Richland and the newer subdivisions, this is the most common cause.

**The fix:** A battery backup sump pump or a water-powered backup. Test the pump every spring by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Replace pumps every 7 to 10 years before they fail. Make sure the discharge goes well away from the foundation.

## 2. Foundation seepage

**How to tell:** Water appearing along the wall-floor joint, through cracks in block or stone walls, or as a damp line at floor level after rain. Efflorescence (white mineral deposits) on the walls.

**Why it happens:** Hydrostatic pressure. Water in the soil around and under the foundation pushes through any opening. Johnstown's stone foundations in Cambria City, Moxham and Kernville and the block foundations of the mid-century neighborhoods are both susceptible, especially on hillside lots where water runs downhill toward the house.

**The fix:** Start outside. Extend downspouts at least six feet from the house. Regrade so soil slopes away from the foundation. Clean gutters. If that is not enough, an interior drain tile system with a sump is the reliable solution in our terrain. Exterior waterproofing works but requires excavation.

## 3. Sewer or drain backup

**How to tell:** Water coming up through the floor drain, a basement toilet or shower drain. Often gray or brown, often smells.

**Why it happens:** The municipal sewer is overwhelmed by rain (common in older combined-sewer neighborhoods), or your lateral line is blocked by roots, grease or collapse.

**The fix:** A backwater valve on your main sewer line, installed by a plumber. A camera inspection of the lateral. And a sewer backup endorsement on your insurance, because standard policies exclude this. Read our [sewage backup guide](/blog/sewage-backup-cleanup-safety/) for cleanup.

## 4. Surface flooding

**How to tell:** Water coming in through window wells, the walkout door, or over the top of the foundation during a storm. Sometimes carrying mud and debris.

**Why it happens:** Rain runs off the hillside, the street or the neighbor's yard faster than the ground can absorb it. Window wells fill. Driveways slope toward the garage. In real flood events, the rivers and creeks come up.

**The fix:** Window well covers and drains, swales or French drains to intercept hillside runoff, driveway trench drains. For river flooding, there is no household fix, only flood insurance and elevation of mechanicals.

## The Johnstown-specific advice

- **Buy flood insurance even outside the mapped zone.** The 1889, 1936 and 1977 floods reached places that are not in today's high-risk zones. Preferred-risk NFIP policies outside the zone are inexpensive.
- **Add a sewer backup endorsement.** Combined sewers in the older parts of the city and boroughs make this one of the most common claims we see.
- **Elevate the furnace, water heater and electrical panel** if you are replacing them anyway.
- **Do not store anything irreplaceable on the basement floor.** Shelving, plastic bins, and nothing in cardboard.
